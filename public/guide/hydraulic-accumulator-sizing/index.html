Hydraulic Accumulator Sizing

The total volume of a bladder/piston accumulator is calculated from the usable oil volume and the working pressures using Boyle's law (isothermal).

⚙ Open the Hydraulic Calculator →

Volume Formula

V0 = ΔV / [ p0 × (1/p1 − 1/p2) ]

All pressures ABSOLUTE (bar abs = gauge + 1.013). Pre-charge p0 ≈ 0.9 × p1.

Units

QuantitySymbolUnit
Total volumeV0L
Usable volumeΔVL
Min/Max pressurep1, p2bar (absolute)
Pre-chargep0bar (absolute)

Worked Example

Given: ΔV = 2 L, min pressure 100 bar, max pressure 160 bar (gauge). Pre-charge p0 = 90 bar gauge.

1) Absolute: p0 = 91.0 · p1 = 101.0 · p2 = 161.0 bar

2) 1/p1 − 1/p2 = 0.00990 − 0.00621 = 0.003689

3) V0 = 2 / (91.0 × 0.003689) = ≈ 6.0 L

Frequently Asked Questions

What should the nitrogen pre-charge be?

Rule of thumb: pre-charge (p0) ≈ 90% of the minimum working pressure. Too low wastes most of the oil; too high empties the accumulator early.

Why use absolute pressure?

Boyle's law (p×V = constant) holds for absolute pressure. Add atmospheric pressure (≈1.013 bar) to the gauge value.

Isothermal or adiabatic?

For slow cycles the isothermal (constant temperature) assumption is adequate. Very fast cycles may need an adiabatic correction (exponent n≈1.4).
