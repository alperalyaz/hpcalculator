Thread Pitch & Size Identification

Two measurements are enough to identify an unknown thread: the major diameter (with calipers) and the pitch (with a pitch gauge). These are matched against standard tables.

Basic Relationship

Metric (60°) minor diameter

dminor ≈ D − 1.0825 × P

Pitch of an inch thread

P = 25.4 / (threads per inch)

D: major diameter (mm), P: pitch (mm)

Worked Example

Given: Calipers read an outer diameter ≈ 20.0 mm; the pitch gauge shows P = 2.5 mm.

1) 20 mm + 2.5 mm pitch → standard match: M20 × 2.5 (metric coarse)

2) Minor diameter ≈ 20 − 1.0825×2.5 = 17.3 mm (as a check)

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I measure the pitch?

The easiest way is a pitch (thread) gauge: find the blade that fits, and it is stamped with the pitch (mm) or TPI. Alternatively, measure across 10 threads and divide by 10.

How do I tell metric from inch?

If the pitch is a round mm value (1.5, 2, 2.5) it is usually metric. If it fits a "threads per inch" value (e.g. 19 TPI) it is a BSP/NPT/UN thread.

What is the difference between BSP and NPT?

BSP can be parallel or tapered and has a 55° thread angle; NPT is tapered and 60°. Mixing them causes leaks.
